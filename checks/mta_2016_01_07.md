# Check: Malware-Traffic-Analysis.net exercise of 2016-01-07

## What this check is

This is an open-answer check. The case was worked with its published answers at hand, so the check shows whether the evidence each answer rests on is present and readable in Glessner. It doesn't show that an analyst who doesn't know the answer would find it. Blind rounds do that, and none has been finished yet. The published answers give 14 items. The check's score is 6, 7, 0 of 13, plus 1 out of scope. Of the 13 items in scope, 6 were reached, 7 were partly reached, and 0 were missed. The 14th, what to do about each host, is out of scope.

## The dataset

- **Source:** [Malware-Traffic-Analysis.net's traffic analysis exercise of 2016-01-07](https://www.malware-traffic-analysis.net/2016/01/07/index.html), titled "Alerts on 3 different hosts", from [Malware-Traffic-Analysis.net](https://www.malware-traffic-analysis.net/).
- **Credit and license:** The exercise, its capture and alert files, and its answers are from Malware-Traffic-Analysis.net. The exercise page states no license. The site's pages carry a copyright line for Malware-Traffic-Analysis.net, and its [about page](https://www.malware-traffic-analysis.net/about.html) says the samples are shared as a resource for threat researchers and other security professionals. This repository holds none of the exercise's files.
- **What it holds:** Network evidence and no host artifacts. Three hosts appear in the traffic: JENNIFER-PC (192.168.122.52), FULL-METAL-JACK (192.168.122.130), and HOKAYDOO-PC (192.168.122.132). The exercise supplies these files:
  - A packet capture, `2016-01-07-traffic-analysis-exercise.pcap`, of 14,184,370 bytes (classic pcap, Ethernet). It covers 22:05:47 to 22:17:18 UTC and holds live malware traffic.
  - Two alert text files from the sensor that watched the same traffic, of 24,009 and 529,780 bytes. One is in Snort's full alert format and holds 82 alerts. The other has "suricata" in its file name, but it is a Sguil (Security Onion) event export of 107 events.
  - The downloads are a capture zip of 12,055,896 bytes and an alerts zip of 80,593 bytes.
- **Where the answers come from:** The exercise's [answers page](https://www.malware-traffic-analysis.net/2016/01/07/page2.html) gives the published answers: its written answer as an image and its details in 24 screenshots of Wireshark and alert output. The 14 answer items below are the facts those pages state.

## How it was run

[glossary.md](../glossary.md) defines terms such as lane, pivot, and mark.

**Date and build.** The check ran on 2026-10-15, on a build that predates v0.1.0, the first build with a version number, and took about an hour. Each gap's state under Gaps found says where the gap stands in v0.2.0, and a fixed gap names the release that fixed it.

**Evidence.** The two zips were downloaded from the exercise page and extracted unchanged. The evidence folder holds copies whose SHA-256 hashes match the originals. The exercise has no host artifacts, so the folder has one top-level folder, `network`, which holds the capture. The two alert files sit under `network/alerts/`.

**Loading.** A case was made from the start page. **Create case and take in evidence** hashed the 3 files in 6 s. The Tools page's **Run and load everything ready** ran Suricata 8.0.7 with ET Open rule set 11310 on the capture and loaded its output, in 9 s in all.

| File | Result | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| The capture | Loaded | Identified by its header. Suricata ran in 6.9 s and the app loaded the output in 0.19 s. The evidence hashes matched after the run. The case holds 1,496 events on one lane, `network`: flow 479, http 302, fileinfo 287, dns 220, tls 126, alert 51, anomaly 20, and dhcp 11. Of the 51 alerts, 42 are severity 1, 3 are severity 2, and 6 are severity 3, so 45 are suggested as Suspicious. |
| The Snort alert file | Not loaded | Listed as unidentified, with no reason given. It is Snort's full alert format: 82 alerts in 474 lines, each over 5 or 6 lines. Its times have no year and are the sensor's local time, an hour ahead of UTC. |
| The "suricata" alert file | Not loaded | Listed as unidentified, with no reason given. It is a Sguil export: 107 events in 8,506 lines, each a block with a signature, addresses, header lines, and a hex payload dump. Its times are UTC, about 28 s early. |

The Tools page counts the two files as "2 unidentified files", with no reason and no link to the Import page. The Import page shows them only after **Show other unidentified files** is ticked. It guesses each is a CSV file with no time field. The ready-made Snort or Suricata fast alert mapping and the mapping for lines that start with a date and time both read 0 records and report "The time field is empty in the records read". The Import button stayed disabled under every mapping.

**Scoring.** Each answer item was looked up in the app with search, the Event page, and pivots, on copies of the case. The work used the app's local HTTP interface and read-only queries of the case database. Small pcap readers ran on copies of the capture.

Each item has one of four states: reached, partly reached, missed, or out of scope. An out-of-scope item needs something the app doesn't do by design, so it's listed and not counted. [How each item is scored](README.md#how-each-item-is-scored) gives the full rubric.

**Marking and the report.** The answers were then marked in the case. 41 events were marked, 35 Malicious and 6 Suspicious, and each mark's note names its answer item. Three more notes carry the per-host conclusions of item 14. The app then wrote the findings summary, the marked-events CSV, and the audit trail from those marks. In v0.2.0 the Markdown report replaces the findings summary. The DHCP acknowledgments were marked Suspicious so that the findings summary would list each host's address and MAC address. The summary lists only Malicious and Suspicious events, and a network-only case has no host to hold the addresses.

### Totals

The score is 6, 7, 0 of 13, plus 1 out of scope. Of the 13 items in scope, 6 were reached (1, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 13), 7 were partly reached (2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 11, and 12), and 0 were missed. Item 14 is out of scope and isn't counted. Of the 3 evidence files, 1 loaded.

Item 9 is reached on its HTTP records, which carry the sizes the answers give. When several records show a fact, the best of them decides, so the file records' truncated sizes are a gap (gap 7) and don't lower the item. The page gives the first run's states.

### What the check showed

- The suggestions, grouped by alert signature, gave the first event for items 8, 9, 10, and 13.
- The Same IP at either end pivot, started from a suggestion, listed one host's activity in time order: 367 events for 192.168.122.130 and 424 for 192.168.122.132.
- Search on a record's data fields reached values that sit only in Raw fields, such as `dhcp.client_mac:*`. **Add to search** from the Event page's Raw fields found all 23 records of one connection through its `flow_id`.
- Flow start times matched the times in the published answers.

### The findings summary

The summary of the checked build listed the 41 marked events in time order. Each had its time to the microsecond, its lane, its mark and notes, the tool and run, the source file and record, the evidence SHA-256, and the event ID. The time, source file, and hash were right on every marked answer, and the summary re-hashed all 3 evidence files. It also showed these problems:

- The host read `network` on all 41 events, and its Hosts table listed only `network`. Which of the three hosts an event concerned showed only as an address in the message (gaps 5 and 10).
- JENNIFER-PC's redirector response and the second Flash request listed last, at 22:17:18.401721, after the payment page (gap 3).
- The MAC addresses, the true download sizes, and the content types reached the summary only through the mark notes (gaps 7 and 8).
- Nothing said that the two alert files, which hold the alerts the capture lacks, were never loaded (gap 11).
- The marked-events CSV had no evidence hash (gap 15), and the audit trail cut event names at 60 characters (gap 16).

## Gaps found

The check found 19 gaps. This page numbers them 1 to 19, in the order the check recorded them, and the results above refer to them by number. Of these, 3 are fixed in v0.2.0, 10 are planned, 1 is superseded, 4 are open, and 1 is out of scope. [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md) lists what v0.2.0 changed, and [known_limitations.md](../known_limitations.md) lists what the app still doesn't do.

A gap's state here is one of five: fixed, with the release that fixed it; planned, where a fix is approved and isn't built; superseded, where another gap's fix covers it, and the state names that gap; open, where the gap is deferred or no fix is chosen yet; or out of scope, where the app leaves it to the analyst by design.

Each gap also has a value and the answer items it affected:

- **Value** is how much fixing the gap is worth: high, medium, or low. Gap 19 is out of scope and has no rating.
- **Items** are the answer items the gap affected.

### Gap 1: The Sguil event export isn't recognized

Value: high. Items: 5, 6, 8, and 11 to 14.

Intake gives no reason, and no import mapping reads a record. The file holds every alert the answers cite that the capture lacks: 2 Neutrino landing alerts and 2 payload alerts, 9 Nemucod Downloading Payload alerts, 9 CryptoWall Check-in alerts, the AlphaCrypt payment page alert, the .onion DNS policy alert, and the three redirector alerts on JENNIFER-PC, from two rules. Its times run 27 to 30 s before the capture's, at one-second resolution.

**State now:** Planned. A reader for Sguil's event text, one event per block without the payload dump, on the sensor's lane. Its clock offset is set by hand, because the clock check compares hosts with the sensor lane, not one sensor with another. Intake will also give an unidentified text file a reason and a link to the Import page. The Planned, not built section of [known_limitations.md](../known_limitations.md#planned-not-built) lists this and gap 2.

### Gap 2: Snort's full alert format isn't recognized

Value: medium. Items: 10 and 12.

The format is Snort's `-A full` output. The fast alert mapping reads 0 of its lines and reports an empty time field, which doesn't say the format is wrong. The file holds 82 alerts, among them the Talos CryptoWall command-and-control rule (4 alerts) and "script before DOCTYPE possible malicious redirect" on the koeppl.com response. Its times have no year and are the sensor's local time, UTC+01:00.

**State now:** Planned. Read the full format in the Snort import, with the year and zone supplied as for fast alerts, and say so when no line fits.

### Gap 3: Records logged at a flow's timeout carry the logging time

Value: high. Items: 5, 6, 11, and 13.

Suricata logs some records when a flow times out, and the app gave them the time of logging, not the time of the traffic. 43 of the 1,496 events (22 file, 15 HTTP, 4 TLS, and 2 alert) sit 12.7 s to 425.7 s after their flow ended, and 33 of them sit at the capture's last packet. A response or file record can then list after later activity, such as the payment page. The app already moved a record logged before its flow began, but not one logged after the flow ended.

**State now:** Planned. Place a record logged at a flow's timeout at the flow's end, or a file record at its HTTP transaction, and keep Suricata's logging time in the details.

### Gap 4: No event holds a host name

Value: high. Items: 2 to 4 and 14.

Suricata's configuration for the run had DHCP extended logging off, so it logged only the server's acknowledgments, which carry no name. The 11 client requests and informs that name all three hosts were never logged. A test run with extended logging on logged 22 DHCP records, including all 11 client messages with a host name.

**State now:** Planned. Turn DHCP extended logging on, and put the host name in the DHCP message and in host identity. A table of the DHCP names, with each one's address, MAC address, time window, and source message, will be the evidence and the default host when you assign flows to a host (gap 5).

### Gap 5: The three hosts never appear as hosts

Value: high. Items: 2 to 4, 7, and 14.

Network-only evidence loads as one lane, `network`. The Hosts panel shows `network` with no names or addresses. The Same host within ±60 s pivot mixes all three hosts: 113 events around the first Nemucod alert and 603 around the redirector. Suggestions group by rule, host, account, or code, not by address, so the 45 Suricata suggestions can't be split per victim.

**State now:** Planned. The sensor lane gets a sublane for each internal address. The analyst assigns selected flows to a host, with the lease evidence, its source, and an undo, and each event keeps its own clock. The design is a first pass. The Planned, not built section of [known_limitations.md](../known_limitations.md#planned-not-built) lists it as network flows assigned to hosts.

### Gap 6: ET Open's disabled rules stay off

Value: medium. Items: 9 and 12.

The app's ET Open rule file keeps the 17,994 rules that ET Open ships disabled switched off. A test run with every rule enabled added 31 alerts: 4 "ET MALWARE CryptoWall Check-in" alerts on the 4 check-ins, 6 "ET ADWARE_PUP Windows executable sent when remote host claims to send an image" alerts on the 6 downloads, and 21 informational alerts.

**State now:** Open. Whether to enable a chosen list, such as malware check-ins and executables sent as images, or to offer the full set, is undecided. Enabling every rule adds noise that the severity 1 and 2 suggestions would raise.

### Gap 7: File records state a truncated size and no type

Value: medium. Items: 9 and 11.

Suricata's 100 KiB limit on HTTP response bodies cut 18 of 287 files at 102,400 bytes, and the file record states that size as the file's size. A test run with the limit off gave the six sizes in the answers. The Windows build of Suricata has no libmagic, so every file reads "type unknown". The HTTP records hold the true size and content type.

**State now:** Open. The options are to lift the body limit for offline captures, to label a truncated file as truncated, and to show the HTTP content type and length when a file has no type. Their effects on the workflow are still to be weighed.

### Gap 8: DHCP and HTTP messages leave out the deciding values

Value: medium. Items: 2 to 5 and 9.

A DHCP message reads "dhcp UDP 192.168.122.1:67 -> 192.168.122.52:68", with no MAC address, assigned address, or message type. HTTP messages have no referrer, content type, or size. The Event page has no Suricata group, so every field sits under Raw fields.

**State now:** Planned. Show the DHCP message type, MAC address, and assigned address in the message, and the host name once gap 4 is fixed. Show the content type and size in HTTP messages. Add a Suricata group to the Event page's details.

### Gap 9: A Suricata record's ID depended on the time zone

Value: medium. Items: all.

A Suricata record's key, and so its event ID, held the time as Suricata wrote it, in the zone of the machine that ran Suricata, such as `2016-01-08T05:05:47.338476+0700` for 2016-01-07 22:05:47 UTC. Two runs of the capture without rules, one in the machine's local zone and one in UTC, gave 1,445 records each and shared 0 keys. A rerun in another zone would have left every mark on a network event attached to nothing.

**State now:** Fixed in v0.2.0. A Suricata record's ID now uses its flow's start in UTC, so a rerun in another time zone keeps the IDs. Opening an older case upgrades it, reloads those events, and records each old ID that the journal named with its new one.

### Gap 10: The host reads `network` for every event

Value: medium. Items: 2 to 14.

The findings summary and the CSV gave `network` as the host of every event, and the Hosts table listed only the sensor. The victim showed only as an address in the message.

**State now:** Superseded by gap 5. Assigned flows will name their real host, so an interim fix in the report won't be built.

### Gap 11: The findings summary didn't say which evidence was never loaded

Value: medium. Items: 5 and 11 to 14.

The summary reported all 3 files re-hashed and listed one run. The 2 unidentified alert files appeared nowhere.

**State now:** Planned. A section of the report lists evidence that wasn't loaded, whether unidentified, skipped, or found but not loaded, each with its reason.

### Gap 12: HTTP and alert records are timed after the response

Value: low. Items: 5, 7, 10, and 12.

HTTP and alert records are timed when Suricata logs the transaction, after the response, not when the request was sent. The gap was 1.6 s for the redirector, 10.5 s for the exploit kit payload, and 51 s for the third check-in, whose server waited. The time description reads only "Suricata http".

**State now:** Planned. The time description will say the record is logged when the response completes, and the details will give the flow's start.

### Gap 13: No pivot joined the records of one connection

Value: low. Items: 5 and 8.

Same source IP and port split a connection by direction (10 of the 23 records of one flow) and, from a response, joined other connections from the same server port. Searching `flow_id:` from **Add to search** worked.

**State now:** Fixed in v0.2.0. A Same flow pivot lists every record with the event's Suricata flow ID.

### Gap 14: The case's first and last event times weren't stated

Value: low. Items: 1.

Neither the Timeline panel nor the findings summary stated the first and last event times of the case. The status line gave counts and timings, and the hover on **Fit case** gave dates only.

**State now:** Planned. Show the span in the status line and state it in the report. In v0.2.0 the Case overview gives each host's first and last time, which for a network-only case is the case's span. The report doesn't state it yet.

### Gap 15: The marked-events CSV lacks the evidence hash and notes

Value: low. Items: 14.

The marked-events CSV has no evidence SHA-256 and leaves out notes, so the three per-host conclusions were only in the findings summary.

**State now:** Planned. Add the evidence SHA-256 and a notes column.

### Gap 16: The audit trail cut event names inside an address

Value: low. Items: all.

The audit trail cut event names at 60 characters, often inside an address, and the mark followed straight after.

**State now:** Fixed in v0.2.0. The audit trail cuts a long event name, at about 100 characters, at a word or address boundary and marks the cut with an ellipsis. The hover shows the name with up to 1,000 characters of the message, and the Markdown export writes the same.

### Gap 17: NetBIOS name registrations load as nameless flows

Value: low. Items: 2 to 4.

NetBIOS name registrations load as nameless UDP 137 flows, because Suricata has no NetBIOS name service parser.

**State now:** Open. Deferred. Fixing gap 4 recovers the same names for this exercise.

### Gap 18: Response content isn't available

Value: low. Items: 6.

Item 6 turns on a 26-byte script, and the app doesn't keep response content.

**State now:** Open. Deferred. File contents are in scope, so the script's text is a fact the app doesn't show, and item 6 is partly reached. Whether to keep small text responses, for example Suricata's stored files in the case folder, is undecided.

### Gap 19: No recommendations at the end of a case

Value: not rated. Items: 14.

There are no recommendations at the end of a case.

**State now:** Out of scope. By design, the app writes none of the report's narrative ([scope.md](../scope.md#not-in-scope)) and no finding or verdict, so the recommendations in item 14 stay with the analyst. v0.2.0 adds Findings, where the analyst writes conclusions with the events that support them. It also adds a note on what some kinds of record don't prove, such as a Prefetch file or a Suricata alert, and an Indicators export from the events marked Malicious.

### Smaller gaps, still open

The check met three more problems outside the numbered gaps. Each is open: no fix is chosen.

- `port:` matches only the source port, so `channel:flow ip:192.168.122.130 port:80` finds nothing. No search term matches a port at either end, as `addr:` does for addresses.
- List rows cut a message at 160 characters. 86 of the 302 HTTP messages and 6 of the 51 alert messages run longer, so the Nemucod GET rows show no client address.
- The Event page lists a network record's addresses and ports under "Account and logon".

## Limits of this check

- The answers were known during the work. The check shows the evidence is present and readable, not that an analyst would find it without the answers.
- After setup, the work went through the app's local HTTP interface, with some pages checked in a browser. How an analyst works by clicking through the page isn't measured.
- The score comes from a build that predates v0.1.0. The page gives the first run's states.
- The answer items are a reading of the answers page, whose written answer is an image and whose details are in screenshots.
- Neither alert file loaded, so the check doesn't show what the Sguil and Snort alerts add once the app reads them.
- The exercise has no host artifacts. Host identity from registry hives, the clock check, and the split by recorded computer had nothing to work on.
- The capture is 14 MB, so the check says nothing about scale.
- Gaps 9, 10, 11, 15, and 16 were found while the findings summary was written, and no second pass checked them. A second pass did check every other gap, with three extra Suricata runs on a copy of the capture: the default configuration, DHCP extended logging with no response-body limit, and every ET Open rule enabled.
- The What has been checked section of [known_limitations.md](../known_limitations.md#what-has-been-checked) says what else hasn't been shown.
